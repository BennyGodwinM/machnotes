Most hobby designs pick a motor from its static thrust chart. That number is measured on a bench at 0 ft/s, and the moment the aircraft moves, it stops being true.

What

Thrust lapse is the drop in available thrust as airspeed or altitude rises. Raymer and Gudmundsson both apply it as a correction to rated thrust before any performance calculation.

How

As forward speed rises, each blade section sees a lower angle of attack and makes less thrust at the same rpm. For a roughly constant-power source, $P = TV$, so

$$ T(V) = \frac{550\,\eta_p\,P}{V} \qquad [T]=\text{lbf},\ [P]=\text{hp},\ [V]=\text{ft/s} $$

That curve blows up as $V \to 0$, so near takeoff the real thrust is capped by the static value $T_0$.

Trap. "The motor makes 4 lbf, so my T/W is 0.8" uses a number that only exists at 0 ft/s. Size climb and cruise with thrust at those speeds.
